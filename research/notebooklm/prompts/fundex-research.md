# Fundex prop-firm research brief

Conduct a source-grounded research study of Fundex using only the sources in this notebook. The result will be reviewed by a human before any information is normalized or published.

Begin by identifying what Fundex actually is. Explain its operating model, whether accounts and execution are simulated or live, how a trader enters, how risk is enforced, and how compensation is calculated and delivered. Keep marketing positioning separate from legal characterization.

Write a coherent English research document. Use explanatory prose, concise fact groups and tables where they improve comparison. Do not write JSON, a database schema or implementation instructions.

## Required sections

1. **Executive brief and how Fundex describes itself**
   - What the project says it is, its value proposition and its own terminology.
   - A plain-language classification of the actual model.
   - The complete trader lifecycle from purchase through evaluation, funded stage, trading and payout.

2. **Programs, challenges and pricing**
   - Identify every current offer and preserve its official name.
   - For every meaningful variant capture account size, fee, number of phases, phase targets, daily loss, maximum drawdown, drawdown type, time limit, minimum trading days, consistency rules, inactivity rules, profit split, refund or activation fees and availability.
   - Include every published pricing tier rather than only minimum and maximum values.
   - Explain balance-versus-equity calculations, reset time, open-PnL treatment, phase resets and breach consequences.

3. **Payouts and trader compensation**
   - Profit split and whether it varies by program or purchase option.
   - First-payout eligibility, cadence, minimum amount, processing language, currency, network and fees.
   - KYC timing, closed-position requirements, partial withdrawals, effect on account balance and drawdown, consistency or profit-day requirements, pending-request trading restrictions and consequences of a later breach.
   - Distinguish guaranteed rules, discretionary approval language, blog examples and user reports.

4. **Trading environment**
   - Terminal, platform, venue, chain and execution model.
   - Exact asset classes and instrument counts when published.
   - Leverage by asset class, spreads, commissions, swap or funding fees and slippage disclosures.
   - News trading, weekend/overnight holding, bots, browser automation, API access, copy trading, hedging, HFT, arbitrage, grid, martingale, mandatory stops, position limits, concurrent positions and multi-account behavior.
   - Explain whether real order-book prices imply live routing or only a simulated price feed.

5. **Legal entity and availability**
   - Legal entity, address, jurisdiction, registration number and product launch date when documented.
   - Founder and public team information.
   - Regulatory positioning and explicit brokerage, custody, advice or financial-product disclaimers.
   - Complete restricted-country and restricted-region list with the source date.
   - Minimum age, KYC stage, governing-law or dispute terms, VPN/IP/device rules and document hierarchy.

6. **Rewards and promotions**
   - Points, tiers, earning actions, multipliers and expiry.
   - Token status, contract, supply, utility and whether it is live or only teased.
   - Airdrop status without converting hints into a guarantee.
   - Referral or affiliate portal, commission schedule, leaderboards, competitions, giveaways, cashback and promo codes.

7. **Transparency and operating statistics**
   - List every meaningful company statistic with exact wording, date and source: funded allocation, trader or user counts, payouts, denial claims, largest/average payout, trading volume and similar metrics.
   - State whether each metric is self-reported, partially evidenced, independently audited or unverifiable.
   - Never equate simulated account allocation with treasury assets, AUM, deployed capital or on-chain TVL.
   - Identify public payout ledgers, on-chain dashboards, smart contracts, proof of reserves and audits, or clearly say when the supplied sources do not establish them.

8. **Reviews and reputation**
   - Include dated ratings and review counts only if the notebook sources support them.
   - Separate recurring positive and negative user-experience themes from factual audit conclusions.
   - Identify whether complaints concentrate on denials, processing time, platform reliability, rule enforcement or another theme.
   - Describe how the company responds to criticism when documented.

9. **Red flags and green flags**
   - Red flags must identify concrete source conflicts, legal discretion, stale claims, technical complaints, short history or missing verification. Do not treat jurisdiction alone as proof of misconduct.
   - Green flags must identify concrete transparency or operating signals, while explaining their limits. A positive review or individual payout is not proof of solvency.

10. **Fundex-specific mechanics**
    - Add dedicated sections for any material mechanism that does not fit the shared structure.
    - Do not force a unique program, reward, execution or risk model into conventional prop-firm terminology.

11. **Sources, conflicts and unresolved questions**
    - List every source used.
    - Preserve conflicting values with their page names and dates.
    - Identify the current canonical rule only when a source hierarchy or newer dated rule supports that choice.
    - End with a concise list of missing facts that should be researched next.

## Evidence rules

- Use only information supported by the notebook sources.
- Prefer current rulebooks, help-center policies, checkout pages and Terms over broad landing-page copy.
- Do not silently resolve contradictions.
- Never convert missing information into zero, false, none or an industry-standard guess.
- Keep announced, roadmap, beta, legacy and live features in different states.
- Clearly distinguish official rules, marketing statements, blog explanations, third-party claims and user reports.
- Keep citations attached to the claims they support.
