What is Fundex?

June 10, 2026

Table of contents

Fundex is a proprietary trading firm that gives skilled traders access to funded accounts without requiring them to risk their own capital. Through a structured evaluation program, traders demonstrate their ability to manage risk and generate consistent returns. Those who qualify are granted a funded account and can earn real payouts based on their trading performance.

​

What makes Fundex different from other prop firms?

​

Fundex operates on an A-Book execution model, meaning trades are mirrored directly in live markets via Bitunix (crypto perps) and Pure Market (CFDs), rather than being held internally. At the funded stage, traders benefit from one of the most flexible rule sets in the industry: no daily drawdown, no trailing drawdown, no minimum holding time, and no consistency rules. Fundex also offers the largest instrument selection of any prop firm, with over 200 crypto pairs alongside forex, stocks, ETFs, commodities, and indices.

What is KYC and why is it required?

KYC stands for Know Your Customer. It is a standard identity verification process used across the financial industry to confirm the identity of users, prevent fraud, and ensure compliance with applicable regulations. KYC verification at Fundex is provided and fully handled by RISE.

​

What documents are needed for KYC?

You will typically need to provide a valid government-issued photo ID, such as a passport or national identity card. Additional documents may be requested depending on your country of residence. All required steps are completed directly through the RISE verification flow, which is accessible inside your Fundex Dashboard.

​

What documents are needed for KYC?

KYC is a mandatory step that must be completed after passing Phase 2 and before your funded account can be activated. It cannot be skipped or deferred. Completing it promptly will ensure there are no delays in getting your funded account live.

​

What happens if I do not complete KYC?

Your funded account will not be activated until KYC is successfully completed. Payout requests cannot be processed without verified identity. If you experience any difficulties during the process, the Fundex support team is available to assist you.

Is Fundex available in all countries?

Fundex is not available in all regions. Certain countries are restricted from accessing the platform due to regulatory requirements, international sanctions, or compliance obligations. Traders residing in restricted countries are not eligible to register, participate in an evaluation, or become funded traders.

​

Which countries are restricted?

The following countries are currently restricted from accessing Fundex services: Pakistan, Afghanistan, Myanmar, Iraq, Somalia, Sudan, South Sudan, Libya, Yemen, UAE, North Korea, Iran, Syria, Cuba, Belarus, and Russia. If your country is not on this list but you are experiencing access issues, please contact the support team for clarification.

​

What happens if I register from a restricted country?

If your country is identified as restricted during the registration or KYC process, your account may be declined or closed. Fundex is unable to offer services in restricted regions regardless of individual circumstances. This is a compliance requirement, not a discretionary decision.

Will I be trading in a live market environment?

During the Fundex evaluation phases, you trade in a simulated environment designed to closely mirror real market conditions. Pricing, spreads, and liquidity reflect what you would encounter in a live setting, making the evaluation a genuine test of your trading ability rather than an artificial exercise.

​

What about once I become a funded trader?

At the funded stage, Fundex manages its own risk on your activity and may hedge or mirror that risk on external venues at its own discretion and for its own account. This is described by the A-Book and B-Book mechanics. Your performance is recorded within the Fundex environment, and it is that recorded performance which determines your eligibility for a Reward. You acquire no interest in, and no entitlement arising from, any position Fundex takes on its own behalf.

Crypto perpetual positions are executed via Bitunix exchange, while CFD positions are executed via MarketMarket.

​

Who provides the pricing and liquidity?

Pricing and liquidity are sourced from Bitunix exchange for crypto perpetuals and from MarketMarket for CFDs. This means the spreads, price movements, and order execution you experience reflect genuine market conditions during both the evaluation and the funded stage.

Account Sharing Detection What Traders Should Know

June 10, 2026

Table of contents

How does Fundex detect account sharing?

Fundex's risk system continuously monitors account activity for signals that suggest an account may be accessed or operated by more than one person. Traders should be aware of the following signals that may trigger a review.

​

Shared hardware ID (CID)

Each device running the trading terminal generates a unique hardware fingerprint. If the same hardware ID is detected across two or more accounts registered to different users, this is treated as a strong indicator of account sharing or use of a shared machine. This signal cannot be masked by changing your IP address or using a VPN.

​

VPS or datacenter IP

If your account is accessed from an IP address associated with a datacenter or VPS server, this suggests the account may be operated remotely, which is typical of copy trading farms or account farming operations. Trading from a VPS or remote server is not permitted.

​

Shared IP address

If multiple accounts are accessed from the same IP address, this may be flagged for review. Please note that a shared IP address alone is not sufficient grounds for a breach. Fundex is aware that mobile carrier networks in many regions use CGNAT, which can cause large numbers of unrelated users to share the same IP. A shared IP will always be assessed alongside other signals before any action is taken.

​

IP address mismatch across multiple countries

If your account is accessed from 3 or more different network ranges across 2 or more countries, this may indicate that the account is being passed between multiple people in different locations.

​

What should I do to avoid triggering these signals?

The simplest way to avoid any flags is to trade exclusively from your own personal device, on your own internet connection, from your usual location. Do not share your login credentials with anyone, do not allow others to access your account, and do not use VPS or remote desktop solutions to operate your account. If you believe your account has been flagged in error, you are welcome to contact the support team to provide context before any action is taken.

Giveaway FAQ.

This FAQ applies only to Giveaway accounts. The 5% payout cap does not apply to purchased accounts.

1. What is the payout cap?

The maximum total amount you can withdraw from a giveaway account is 5% of the account’s initial balance.

This applies to all account sizes.

Examples:

* $5,000 account → $250 maximum payout

* $10,000 account → $500 maximum payout

* $25,000 account → $1,250 maximum payout

* $50,000 account → $2,500 maximum payout

* $100,000 account → $5,000 maximum payout

* $200,000 account → $10,000 maximum payout

2. Can I withdraw more than once?

Yes. You can withdraw once, twice, or multiple times.

However, the combined total of all withdrawals cannot exceed 5% of the account’s initial balance.

3. Is the 5% cap per withdrawal?

No. The 5% cap is the maximum cumulative payout for the account.

For example, on a $5,000 account, you could withdraw:

* $100 on your first payout

* $75 on your second payout

* $75 on your third payout

Total withdrawn: $250

You cannot withdraw more than $250 in total.

4. Does the 5% cap reset after each payout?

No. The cap does not reset after a payout.

Once you withdraw an amount, it is deducted from your total 5% payout allowance.

5. What happens if I make more than 5% profit?

You can continue trading according to the applicable account rules, but the maximum total amount you can withdraw remains capped at 5% of the initial account balance.

​

6. How is my maximum payout calculated?

The payout cap is calculated based on the initial account balance, not your current balance, maximum total payout = 5% × Initial Account Balance.

7. Are giveaway accounts eligible for Monthly Salary?

No. Giveaway accounts are not eligible for the Monthly Salary program.

This applies regardless of the account size.

How Many Phases Are There?

How is the evaluation structured?

The Fundex evaluation follows a two-phase process. Phase 1 requires you to reach an 8% profit target while staying within the account rules. Once you pass Phase 1, you move on to Phase 2, which carries a 5% profit target under the same risk parameters. Successfully completing both phases makes you eligible for a funded account.

​

Do my results from Phase 1 carry over into Phase 2?

No. Each phase begins with a fresh account balance equal to your original starting balance. Your trading history and performance from Phase 1 do not carry over into Phase 2. Each phase is assessed independently.

​

Are the rules the same across both phases?

Yes. The risk management rules, including the daily loss limit, maximum drawdown, minimum holding time, and minimum trading days, apply equally across both phases. The only difference between the phases is the profit target.

What is the maximum drawdown limit?

The maximum drawdown limit is set at 10% of your starting balance and remains fixed for the entire duration of your evaluation. It does not trail your equity and does not change if your account grows. For example, if you start with a $100,000 evaluation account, your account equity must never reach or fall below $90,000 at any point during the evaluation.

​

Is the maximum drawdown calculated on open or closed positions?

The maximum drawdown is tracked on an equity basis, which means it accounts for both your open and closed positions at all times. Floating losses from open trades count toward this limit throughout the trading session.

​

Does the maximum drawdown reset between phases?

Yes. Since each phase begins with a fresh account balance equal to your original starting balance, the maximum drawdown floor resets accordingly at the start of Phase 2.

What is the daily loss limit?

The daily loss limit is set at 5% of your account balance and applies across both Phase 1 and Phase 2. Unlike the maximum drawdown, this limit is recalculated once per day at the daily reset, which occurs at 00:00 UTC. The calculation is based on your account balance at the time of the reset, not your original starting balance.

​

Can you give an example of how the daily loss limit works?

If your account balance at the daily reset is $100,000, your daily loss limit for that session is $5,000, meaning your equity must not fall below $95,000 during that trading day. If you close the day at $110,000, your new daily loss floor after the next reset becomes $104,500.

​

Does the daily loss limit include open positions?

Yes. The daily loss limit is tracked on an equity basis and accounts for floating losses from open positions throughout the trading day, not just closed results.

Is there a minimum number of trading days required?

Yes. Both Phase 1 and Phase 2 require a minimum of 5 trading days to be completed before the phase can be passed. A day counts toward this requirement only if you have opened at least one trade with a notional size equal to or greater than 5% of your account balance before leverage. The minimum trading day requirement encourages a measured, consistent approach to trading. It helps ensure that evaluation results reflect genuine trading behaviour over a reasonable period rather than a single concentrated session.

Holding Time Rule

Table of contents

Is there a minimum trade holding time during the evaluation?

Yes. During both evaluation phases, all trades must be held for a minimum of 3 minutes before being closed. This rule applies to both Phase 1 and Phase 2. At the funded stage, there is no minimum holding time.

​

Does the 3-minute rule apply if my Take Profit or Stop Loss is hit before 3 minutes?

Yes. The minimum holding time applies regardless of how a trade is closed, including when a Take Profit or Stop Loss is triggered automatically before the 3-minute mark. This is intentional. Without this, the rule could easily be bypassed by placing a TP or SL at or near the current market price to force an instant close. If you are concerned about a position moving against you quickly, we recommend sizing your trades appropriately and placing your Stop Loss at a level that gives the trade enough room to develop over at least 3 minutes.

​

What happens if I close a trade before the 3-minute minimum?

A first violation of the holding time rule results in a warning only. Your account will not be immediately breached. Any further violations following the warning will result in your account being breached, so we kindly ask that you ensure all trades are held for a minimum of 3 minutes before being closed.

​

Why does Fundex have a minimum holding time during the evaluation?

The 3-minute minimum holding time is in place to encourage deliberate, well-considered trading decisions. It is designed to discourage strategies that rely on exploiting very short-term price inefficiencies or platform latency rather than genuine market analysis. The rule helps ensure that evaluation results reflect a trader's real ability to read and respond to market conditions in a sustainable way.

What is a consistency rule?

A consistency rule is a requirement used by many prop firms that limits how much of your total profit can come from a single trading day. Typically, no single day's result may represent more than a fixed percentage of your overall gains. In practice, this means that even if you have a legitimately strong trading day, you can be penalised for it or prevented from passing your evaluation as a result.

​

Does Fundex have a consistency rule?

No. Fundex does not impose a consistency rule at any stage. You are free to trade your best on any given day without concern that a strong session will work against you.

​

Why does Fundex not have a consistency rule?

Fundex does not have a consistency rule because we believe it penalises good trading rather than protecting against bad trading. Markets are not uniform. Some days offer better conditions than others, and a skilled trader should be free to capitalise on those opportunities without restriction. More importantly, Fundex operates on an A-Book execution model, meaning funded traders are mirrored in live markets. We have no incentive to see traders fail. Our business grows when our traders succeed, which is why our program is designed to assess genuine trading ability rather than introduce unnecessary obstacles.

What constitutes a breach during the evaluation?

A breach occurs if your account equity reaches or falls below either the maximum drawdown limit or the daily loss limit at any point during the evaluation. A breach results in the immediate failure of your current evaluation phase.

​

Can I recover from a breach?

Once an evaluation phase has been breached, it cannot be recovered or reinstated. To continue, you would need to purchase a new evaluation and restart the process from Phase 1.

Self-copy trading refers to operating two or more Fundex accounts simultaneously and replicating the same trades across them, whether manually, through an automated tool, or through any other method of trade mirroring. How is self-copy trading detected?

RiskDesk continuously monitors trading activity across all accounts on the platform. Self-copy trading is flagged when the system detects a consistent pattern of matching trades across multiple accounts, including same instrument and direction, identical or near-identical position sizes, entries opened within seconds of each other, and this pattern repeating across multiple sessions and trading days.

All accounts involved are subject to termination. Profits generated through coordinated activity are subject to review and may be withheld. What counts as gambling behaviour?

Fundex evaluates not just your results, but how you achieve them. A profitable account can still be flagged if the trading behaviour underlying those results does not reflect a structured, repeatable approach. We fund traders, not gamblers.

What behaviours are considered gambling?

Over-leveraging and overexposure: committing a disproportionately large portion of your account equity to a single trade, or holding excessive combined exposure across your open positions at the same time. Even when such a trade wins, systematic over-leveraging demonstrates a risk profile that Fundex cannot support at scale. Exact exposure limits are set out below.

Directionless trading: entering positions without clear analytical reasoning. This includes impulsive trades, random entries, and revenge trading, which means opening positions immediately after a loss with the intent of recovering quickly rather than following a strategy.

Habitual one-sided bets: repeatedly entering large positions in the same direction without a structured rationale, relying on luck or momentum alone rather than analysis.

Overtrading in short windows: opening an excessive number of trades within a very short period. Volume alone does not constitute a strategy, and patterns of this kind suggest emotional or erratic decision-making rather than disciplined execution.

How exposure limits work

The clearest and most measurable form of over-leveraging is position exposure. Exposure is measured as notional value (position size multiplied by price) relative to your account equity, independently of the leverage available on the instrument. Leverage affects how much margin is locked to open a position, but it does not change the position's exposure to the market, so limits are applied to notional value.

Position exposure (%) = (Position size × Entry price) / Account equity × 100 Total exposure (%) = (Combined notional of all open positions) / Account equity × 100

The following thresholds apply.

Single position (all stages):

Up to 25% of equity — compliant

25% to 40% of equity — warning, flagged

Above 40% of equity — violation, subject to review

Total simultaneous exposure:

Evaluation stage: up to 50% compliant, 50% to 80% warning, above 80% violation

Funded stage: above 60% is treated as a violation

The funded-stage total limit is tighter because real risk is managed against your activity, and sustained high exposure can lead to liquidation-level losses on an adverse move.

Example, on a $100,000 account: a single position of $21,000 notional (21%) is compliant; $35,000 (35%) is flagged; $480,000 (480%) is a violation regardless of whether it wins. Three positions open at once worth $30,000, $25,000 and $40,000 total 95% of equity, exceeding the total limit.

What happens if gambling behaviour is detected?

Fundex monitors trading behaviour on an ongoing basis. Accounts flagged for gambling behaviour may receive a warning, be placed under review, or be terminated depending on the severity and frequency of the pattern. Isolated events are treated differently from repeated or sustained patterns; the focus is on identifying a consistent pattern rather than penalising a single trade. Rewards generated through gambling behaviour are subject to review and may be withheld.

Are there specific trading strategies that are not allowed?

Fundex is built for real, disciplined traders. Certain strategies are prohibited not because they are necessarily unprofitable, but because they exploit platform mechanics, create unmanageable risk exposure, or do not reflect genuine market engagement.

Which strategies are strictly prohibited?

Martingale: any system that increases position size after a losing trade with the intent of recovering losses. This creates exponential risk exposure and is considered an abuse of the funding model.

Grid trading: placing a series of buy and sell orders at fixed price intervals regardless of market direction or analysis.

Tick trading and latency arbitrage: strategies that exploit ultra-short price movements or delays between data feeds. These do not reflect real market conditions and are not replicable in a live institutional environment.

High-Frequency Trading (HFT): automated strategies that execute a disproportionately high number of trades in very short timeframes, generating artificial volume without directional intent.

Position stacking: opening 3 or more positions in the same direction on the same instrument at the same time. This is treated as a single oversized trade disguised across multiple entries.

Averaging down: systematically adding to a losing position as price moves further against you, with no predefined exit logic.

What happens if a prohibited strategy is detected?

Accounts found to be using prohibited strategies may be permanently closed. Fundex reserves the right to void any profits generated through these methods and deny payout accordingly.

What is the difference between A-Book and B-Book execution?

In an A-Book approach, a trader's activity is offset externally rather than held against them, so the firm is not positioned on the opposite side of the trader. There is no structural conflict between the trader's success and the firm's interests.

In a B-Book approach, the firm keeps the risk of a trader's activity internally, acting as the counterparty. This means the firm can benefit when a trader loses, which creates a structural conflict of interest. B-Book models are common in the prop trading industry, and are often why certain firms benefit from traders failing their evaluations.

How does Fundex use these models?

Fundex manages its own risk on an A-Book basis while your funded account is at or above its starting balance, so that our interests stay aligned with yours rather than against you. How Fundex chooses to hedge, route or hold that risk on any external venue at any given moment is determined by Fundex at its sole discretion and for its own account, as set out in clause 3 of the Terms. Your account's balances and results are recorded within the Fundex environment, and your eligibility for a Reward is calculated from those recorded metrics. For a full breakdown of how the A-Book and B-Book mechanics work across all possible scenarios, visit fundex.gg/how-it-works.

​

A B-Book state also exists within the Fundex system, but it functions as a temporary risk-management buffer rather than a permanent execution model.

What Triggers a Switch to B-Book?

Updated this week

Table of contents

When does my account switch from A-Book to B-Book?

If your funded account balance drops 1% below its original starting balance while you have open positions, all positions are automatically closed and your account temporarily switches to B-Book mode. This threshold is always calculated from your original starting balance, not your current balance. For example, on a $100,000 account, the switch triggers if your balance falls below $99,000, regardless of whether you have previously built your balance above $100,000.

Does switching to B-Book mean I have failed?

No. Switching to B-Book is not a breach and does not result in account closure. It is a temporary state. You can continue trading in B-Book mode with the goal of recovering your balance back to the original starting level, at which point your account returns to A-Book automatically.

How many times can this happen before it becomes a breach?

Each time your account switches to B-Book and you fail to recover to your original starting balance before triggering another 1% drop, a counter is incremented. Four consecutive unrecovered switches result in a permanent breach. Here is an example on a $100,000 account: Switch 1 balance drops below $99,000, counter 1/4. Switch 2 balance drops below $99,000 again without recovery, counter 2/4. Switch 3 counter 3/4. Switch 4 account is permanently breached, counter 4/4. The counter resets only when you have fully cleared the accumulated offset from all prior switches.

The A-Book and B-Book mechanics are part of the Fundex simulated environment and describe how FUNDEX manages its own risk. Balances and equity are performance metrics, not a deposit, and have no monetary value outside that calculation. Whether and how Fundex hedges or routes its own risk on any venue is at its sole discretion and for its own account, as set out in clause 3 of the Terms.

How does my account return to A-Book execution?

Once you are in B-Book mode, you need to trade your balance back up to your original starting balance. That figure never changes regardless of how much you have previously withdrawn or earned. When your balance reaches the original starting level, your account switches back to A-Book automatically and a 1% offset is applied, representing the amount that needs to be recovered before any Reward becomes payable.

What is the offset and how does it work?

The offset is a running total that reflects the cumulative amount your account has dropped below its original starting balance across all B-Book episodes. It must be fully cleared before any Reward share applies to your closed trades. For example on a $100,000 account: Episode 1 — balance drops to $98,500 before recovery. Offset added: $1,000. Episode 2 — balance drops again before the offset is cleared. A further $1,000 is added. Total offset outstanding: $2,000. You need to generate $2,000 in closed profits above your starting balance before Reward share begins to apply.

Can I receive a Reward while I have an outstanding offset?

Profit share is not applied to trades closed while an offset remains outstanding. You can continue trading during offset recovery, but the 30% profit share on A-Book trades will only begin once the full accumulated offset has been cleared.

What causes a permanent breach at the funded stage?

There are two conditions that result in a permanent breach. The first is four consecutive unrecovered B-Book switches. Example on a $100,000 account: Switch 1 balance drops below $99,000, no recovery, counter 1/4. Switch 2 balance drops below $99,000 again, counter 2/4. Switch 3 counter 3/4. Switch 4 account permanently breached, counter 4/4. The second breach condition is a 10% drop from your original starting balance while in B-Book mode. On a $100,000 account this means your balance reaching or falling below $90,000 while in B-Book, resulting in an immediate permanent breach regardless of the counter.

Is there any way to recover from a funded stage breach?

A permanent breach at the funded stage cannot be reversed. The support team is available to assist you with your options going forward.

How does the profit split work?

At the funded stage, you keep 70% of all eligible profits generated on your account. Fundex takes the remaining 30%. This split applies to closed profits generated while your account is on A-Book and your balance is above its original starting balance, with no outstanding offset.

When can I request a reward?

Reward requests can be submitted at any time once you have generated eligible profits of at least $50. There are no fixed reward windows, no waiting periods, and no minimum number of trading days required at the funded stage.

How often can I withdraw?

As frequently as you like. Once your available Reward balance reaches the $50 minimum threshold, you can submit a withdrawal request. There is no cap on how many requests you can make.

Is there a maximum Reward amount?

No. You can withdraw your full available Reward balance at any time, provided the $50 minimum threshold is met. There is no upper limit on individual Reward amounts.

What counts as eligible profit?

Eligible profit refers to gains generated while your account is on A-Book and your balance is above its original starting balance. Any outstanding offset from prior B-Book episodes must be fully cleared before profit share calculations begin. Once the offset is cleared, the 70/30 split applies to all subsequent A-Book gains.

How Does A-Book Execution Work in Practice?

June 10, 2026

Table of contents

What does A-Book execution mean for me as a trader?

Under the A-Book model, your trades are not held internally by Fundex. Instead, when you open a position on your funded account, Fundex mirrors that trade in live markets. Crypto perpetual positions are executed via Bitunix exchange, while CFD positions are executed via Pure Market. Your profits come from real market outcomes rather than from the firm's internal book. For a full breakdown of how the A-Book execution model works across all possible scenarios, visit fundex.gg/how-it-works.

Why does this matter compared to other prop firms?

Many prop trading firms operate on a B-Book model, where the firm acts as the counterparty to your trades. In that structure, the firm profits when you lose, which creates a direct conflict of interest. Under Fundex's A-Book model, there is no such conflict. The firm earns its share of profit only when you do, which means your success and Fundex's success are genuinely aligned.

Are there any trading fees on Fundex?

Yes. Trading fees apply on the Fundex terminal as trades are executed directly on Bitunix exchange for crypto perpetuals. Since execution is routed through Bitunix, the fees follow Bitunix's standard fee structure. These are not fees imposed by Fundex.

What are the fees?

The fees on the Fundex terminal are as follows: Maker fee: 0.02%. Taker fee: 0.06%. These are standard centralised exchange fees, in line with what you would pay trading directly on any major crypto exchange.

What is the difference between a maker and a taker fee?

A maker fee applies when you place a limit order that is not immediately filled, adding liquidity to the order book. A taker fee applies when you place a market order or a limit order that fills immediately, removing liquidity from the order book. Using limit orders where possible will result in lower fees.

Do fees apply to CFD instruments as well?

CFD instruments executed via Pure Market may have their own fee structure. For the most current information on CFD trading costs, please refer to the Fundex Help Center or contact the support team.
